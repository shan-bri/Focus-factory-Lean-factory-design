AeroCell: Focused Factory Simulator

The brief

AeroCell Components Ltd (a fictional company) is a tier-1 supplier making machined and sheet-metal detail parts for the wing, flaps and landing gear of a regional airliner. The factory uses a process layout: machines are grouped by type, and batches move between departments by forklift.

The customer has asked for shorter lead times and less stock in the supply chain. Today the plant releases one batch per part per week. Average lead time is over three working days, about 1,800 parts are in work, and forklifts make more than 60 trips a week.

Your task: redesign the plant as a focused factory. Form part families, build cells on the shop floor, choose a batch size, and prove with the simulator that your design performs better.

  1. Part families: use the routing matrix (and BOA) to group the 14 parts into families. Each family will be made in its own cell.
  2. Build the factory: move machines into cells and tag each one with its cell. Buy extra machines if a cell needs them. The furnace and anodise line cannot be moved.
  3. Simulate: run four weeks of production and compare your design with the current factory.
  4. Improve and debrief: iterate, record your runs, and answer the debrief questions.

How the model works

  • The plant works 2 shifts × 8 h, 5 days a week. The model runs 4 weeks; week 1 is a warm-up and is excluded from lead time and WIP.
  • Each part is released as a batch every N days of demand (you choose N). Batches queue first-in, first-out at each machine.
  • Setup: no setup if the machine last ran the same part; a short setup (15%) if it last ran a part of the same technological family; otherwise a full setup.
  • Moves: machines within 10 m (2 tiles) can pass parts by hand (about 2 min). Longer moves need a forklift: about 45 min average wait plus travel.
  • One-piece flow: when consecutive operations are in the same cell and within 10 m, the next machine can start as soon as the first part arrives.
  • A part uses machines in its own cell first, then shared machines, then machines in other cells (inter-cell moves).
  • The furnace takes up to 4 loads at once (4 h cycle); the anodise line takes 3 loads (90 min cycle).

Debrief

Answer with evidence from your run history.

  1. Which part families did you form, and which parts were exceptional elements? How did you deal with each one (shared machine, extra machine, accept inter-cell moves)?
  2. Run your cell layout with weekly batches, then with daily batches. Run the current factory with daily batches. Which change gave most of the improvement, and why?
  3. Why can a focused factory run smaller batches than the process layout? Use the setup hours and utilisation figures.
  4. The furnace and anodise line could not move. What form of focused factory does that create for those operations (flow line, workcell or workcenter)? How did their position affect your layout?
  5. Which machines have low utilisation in your design? Is that a problem? Link to the disadvantages of focused factories from the lecture.
  6. Was every machine you bought worth it? Compare the improvement with the capital spend.
  7. Name two things this model leaves out that would matter in a real aerospace plant (for example quality escapes, operator skills, first-article inspection, certification).
